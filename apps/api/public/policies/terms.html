9. How search results and rankings work

The law now requires an online marketplace to explain, in plain and intelligible language, the main factors that determine the order in which products and sellers are presented, and the relative importance of those factors. We think this is a sensible requirement and we set it out here honestly.

The order in which shops and products appear to you is determined principally by the following, in approximately descending order of weight:

  1. Whether the shop is currently open and delivering. A shop that is closed, or that has paused deliveries, is ranked below one that can actually fulfil your order, because a listing you cannot buy from is of no use to you.
  2. How close the shop is to your delivery address. VillKro is a hyperlocal service and proximity directly determines how quickly an order can reach you, so distance carries substantial weight.
  3. Whether the item is in stock. Items a vendor has marked unavailable are demoted or hidden.
  4. The relevance of the item to what you searched for, or to the category you are browsing. This is straightforward text matching against the product name, its category and its description.
  5. Your own order history. Items and shops you have ordered from before may appear higher, on the assumption that you are more likely to want them again.

Payment by a vendor is not one of these factors. A vendor cannot buy a higher position in ordinary search results or category listings. The only circumstance in which payment affects placement is a sponsored listing, and as section 8 explains, a sponsored listing is always labelled as such. We also do not manipulate search results or the search index in order to mislead you about what is available or about how popular or well regarded something is.

When you tap to place an order, you are making an offer to buy the listed goods from the vendor on the terms displayed. At that point the order sits in the status Pending. No contract of sale exists yet. The vendor then either accepts the order or declines it. The contract of sale is formed at the moment the vendor accepts.

A vendor may decline an order, and this is not unusual. The most common reasons are that an item has sold out since the listing was last updated, that the shop has closed for the day, or that the vendor cannot prepare the order within a reasonable time. If a vendor declines, we will notify you and the order will be cancelled. Nothing will be charged to you, because you will not have paid.

We may also decline or cancel an order ourselves. We do this where the delivery address turns out to be outside a service area or cannot be reached, where we have reasonable grounds to suspect fraud or misuse of a promotional offer, where an obvious pricing error has occurred, or where we are required to by law. Where we do this we will tell you the reason.

That general position does not apply, and you are entitled to a remedy, in any of the following situations:

  • The goods are damaged, crushed, leaking or broken.
  • The goods are spoiled, stale, or otherwise not fit to eat.
  • The goods are past their expiry date or “use by” date, or so close to it that they cannot reasonably be used.
  • You have been sent the wrong item, or the wrong variant of the right item.
  • The quantity or weight delivered is short of what you ordered and paid for.
  • An item you ordered and paid for is missing from the delivery.
  • The goods do not match the description or the declarations shown in the listing.

We will investigate with the vendor and then offer you a replacement, the supply of the missing quantity, or a refund, depending on what went wrong and on what you would prefer. Where the complaint is clear on its face, we will not put you through a protracted process.

15. Our commitment against dark patterns

A “dark pattern” is a design choice in an interface that is deliberately built to push, mislead or trick a user into doing something they did not intend to do — buying something they did not want, paying more than they realised, or surrendering more than they meant to. In November 2023 the Central Consumer Protection Authority issued the Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, which identify thirteen specific practices and prohibit them in consumer-facing platforms in India.

We are committed to those Guidelines, and we state plainly that we do not employ any of the prohibited practices:

Prohibited practiceWhat it means, and our position
False urgencyInventing scarcity or a countdown to rush a decision. We do not display stock levels or timers that are not genuine.
Basket sneakingSlipping an extra item, fee, donation or protection plan into the basket. Nothing enters your basket unless you put it there.
Confirm shamingUsing guilt or shame to steer a choice, such as a decline button reading “No, I don't care about saving money”. We use neutral wording.
Forced actionRequiring an unrelated purchase, sign-up or permission in order to continue. Declining the location permission, for instance, does not block you from ordering.
Subscription trapMaking a subscription easy to start and hard to cancel. VillKro has no subscriptions at all.
Interface interferenceDesigning the screen to hide or de-emphasise the option the user actually wants. Cancel, decline and delete are presented legibly.
Bait and switchAdvertising one thing and delivering another. Where a vendor does this, the listing is removed.
Drip pricingRevealing charges gradually through checkout. The full amount payable is shown before you confirm.
Disguised advertisementPresenting paid placement as an ordinary result. Sponsored listings, if any, are clearly labelled.
NaggingRepeatedly interrupting with the same request. We do not re-prompt for a permission you have already declined.
Trick wordingDeliberately confusing phrasing, such as a double negative on a consent checkbox.
SaaS billingExtracting recurring payments through concealed means. Not applicable; we have no recurring billing.

We audit the application against these Guidelines once every year and publish the resulting compliance certificate, as the amended E-Commerce Rules require from 1 January 2027. The first certificate will be published on this page once the first audit is complete.

If you encounter anything in the application that you feel pushed or tricked you into a choice you did not want to make, we would genuinely like to know. Report it under section 19 and we will treat it as a formal grievance rather than as a suggestion.
