3. Location data, explained in full

Location is the most sensitive category of information the application handles, and the rules that apply to it — both in law and under the Google Play developer policies — are correspondingly strict. We therefore deal with it separately and in detail, and we distinguish between the two very different ways it is used.

If you are a customer

We use your precise location only while the application is open and on your screen, and only after you have granted the location permission. It is used for three things: to fill in your address automatically when you are adding a new one, so that you do not have to type it; to show you the shops that are near enough to deliver to you; and to display your order moving towards you on the map while it is being delivered.

We do not collect a customer's location in the background. We do not collect it when the application is closed or minimised. We do not build or retain a history of the places you have been, and we could not reconstruct your movements from what we hold.

You are free to refuse. If you decline the location permission, the application continues to work normally. You simply set your delivery address by dropping a pin on the map yourself, which takes a few seconds longer. We do not block the service, nag you repeatedly, or degrade the experience in order to pressure you into granting it — doing so would be the practice known as forced action, which is prohibited.

If you are a VillKro delivery partner (rider)

Delivery partners' location is collected in the background. We want to state this as plainly as possible rather than leave it buried. When you are signed in as a delivery partner, have marked yourself as online, and have an active delivery assigned to you, the application collects your device's location in the background — that is, continuously, including while the application is minimised, in the background, or closed.

The purpose is specific and limited: so that the customer waiting for the order and the shop that handed it over can see the delivery moving on a map in real time, so that the order can be routed sensibly, and so that completion of the delivery can be confirmed. Live tracking is one of the core features of the service and it cannot be provided without it.

This collection begins only after you have been shown a separate disclosure screen explaining it in these terms and have agreed to it, and after Android has separately asked you for the background location permission. It runs only while you are online with an active delivery, and it stops as soon as you go offline, complete the delivery, or sign out. Whenever it is running, a persistent notification is displayed on your phone so that you always know it is active. Location points are retained only for the life of the delivery. We do not use them to monitor you outside active deliveries, and we do not use them to assess or profile you as an individual.

Whether you are a customer or a delivery partner, you can revoke the location permission at any time from your device's Settings application, without asking us and without any consequence for your account beyond the loss of the features that depend on it.

4. Crash and error diagnostics

The application uses Google Firebase Crashlytics to report crashes and errors back to us. It is disabled in development builds and enabled in the builds released to the app stores. We want to describe exactly what it sends, because “we collect diagnostic data” is the kind of phrase that can conceal a great deal.

When the application crashes, or encounters an error it can recover from, Crashlytics transmits the following: the error itself and its stack trace, which is the technical record of what the software was doing at the moment it failed; your numeric VillKro account identifier and your role on the platform, meaning whether you are a customer, a delivery partner or a shop owner; the version and build number of the application; the name of the screen you were on; whether the device was online at the time; the shop being viewed, if any; and standard device information such as the model, the operating system version, and an installation identifier generated by Crashlytics.

It deliberately does not transmit your name, your telephone number, your addresses, the contents of your cart or order, or the body or headers of any network request or response. That last exclusion is deliberate and specific: request and response bodies would otherwise carry telephone numbers and addresses into the crash reports, so they are excluded at the point of capture rather than filtered afterwards.

We should be clear about the distinction between diagnostics and analytics, because it matters. This is diagnostics: it exists so that when the application breaks we can find out why and fix it. It is not analytics and it is not tracking. We do not use Google Analytics, Firebase Analytics, any advertising or attribution software development kit, any behavioural analytics product, or any cross-application or cross-site tracking technology. Nothing in the application is instrumented to study your behaviour or to build a profile of you.

The first is your consent, which you give by ticking the acceptance box when you create your account, and separately by granting the location and notification permissions your device asks you for. Consent under the Act must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and given by a clear affirmative action; it must be as easy to withdraw as it was to give; and it can only cover the specific purposes described in the notice. Those constraints have shaped how this policy is written.

The second is the set of legitimate uses set out in section 7 of the Act, which permit processing without consent in defined circumstances. The relevant one for us is compliance with a legal obligation: where a law requires a company to retain a record, we retain it, and we cannot delete it on request. The clearest example is the invoice and payment records described in section 8.

6. Children and the age limit

VillKro is available only to people aged 18 and over, and we do not knowingly collect the personal data of anyone below that age.

The reason is set out in section 5 of the Terms of Service and is worth repeating here. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, a “child” means any person who has not completed eighteen years of age. The Act draws that line without graduated age bands and without exceptions for particular kinds of service. Processing a child's personal data requires verifiable consent from a parent or lawful guardian, which is a demanding standard involving genuine verification of the adult's identity and of their relationship to the child, and the Act separately prohibits tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children in all circumstances.

Rather than construct and operate that verification machinery for a grocery delivery service, we have chosen to place the service out of scope for children altogether. We consider this both simpler and more protective.

We do not track, monitor, profile or direct any advertising at any user, and certainly not at a child. If we become aware that an account has been created by a person under 18, we will close the account and delete the associated personal data. If you are a parent or guardian and you believe a child is using VillKro, please write to decodelabsofficial@gmail.com and we will act on it without delay.

8. How long we keep data, and deletion

We keep personal data only for as long as the purpose set out in section 2 actually requires, or for as long as a law obliges us to retain it. When neither applies any longer, it is deleted.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account yourself, at any time, from inside the application at Profile → Delete Account. No reason is required and you do not have to ask us. This is what then happens, in order:

  1. Your account is marked for deletion immediately and you are signed out on every device.
  2. A thirty-day grace period begins. During it you can reverse the decision by signing in and choosing Cancel deletion in your Profile, and nothing will have been lost. This period exists for a specific reason: account deletion is irreversible, and an accidental tap, or a deletion made under pressure from another person, should be recoverable. We think the ability to change your mind is worth more than the extra thirty days of retention costs you.
  3. After thirty days, an automated job that runs daily permanently erases the account. It removes your name, telephone number, sign-in identity, password hash, WhatsApp number, saved addresses, cart, sessions and push tokens.
  4. Encrypted backups that still contain the deleted data are rotated out and destroyed within a further ninety days, in the ordinary course of our backup cycle.

If you cannot get into the application, write to decodelabsofficial@gmail.com from your registered e-mail address, stating your registered telephone number so that we can verify the request, and we will complete the deletion within thirty days. The whole process is described step by step on our Account and Data Deletion page.

One important qualification, stated plainly

If you have ever placed an order, your account record is anonymised rather than deleted outright. Indian tax and company law requires a business to retain its sales and invoice records for several years, and an order record cannot be detached from the customer row it belongs to without destroying the accounting record itself.

What we do instead is strip the record of everything that identifies you: your name is replaced with “Deleted User”, your telephone number is replaced with a meaningless internal reference, and your password hash, sign-in identity, WhatsApp number, addresses and push tokens are erased entirely. What survives is the commercial fact of the order — what was bought, from which shop, for how much, and when — no longer attached to anything that identifies you as a person. It is used for accounting only, and it cannot be used to contact you or to recognise you if you return. If you never placed an order, no such record exists and your user record is deleted outright.

10. How we protect your data

Section 8(5) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and Rule 6 of the Rules made under it require a Data Fiduciary to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach. These are the measures we have in place.

  • Encryption in transit. All traffic between the application and our servers travels over HTTPS with TLS. Data is not transmitted in plain text at any point.
  • Passwords are never stored. Only a bcrypt hash is kept. Bcrypt is a deliberately slow, salted hashing function designed to resist brute-force attack. The original password cannot be recovered from the hash, by us or by anyone else.
  • Restricted database access. Access to the production databases is limited to the application server and to named administrators, granted on the basis of what a person actually needs in order to do their work. Database credentials are not distributed beyond that.
  • Authentication by one-time code. Sign-in is verified by a code sent to your registered mobile number, and sessions expire rather than lasting indefinitely.
  • Rate limiting. Our programming interfaces are rate limited, which blunts automated abuse, password-guessing and credential-stuffing attacks.
  • Logging and monitoring. Security logs are retained for one year so that an incident can be investigated and reconstructed.
  • Backups. Encrypted backups are taken regularly and rotated, so that data can be restored after a failure.
  • Contractual obligations on processors. Every processor listed in section 7 is contractually required to maintain equivalent safeguards.

We should be honest about the limits of any such list. No system connected to the internet can be made perfectly secure, and anyone who tells you otherwise is overstating their case. What we can say is that we take the obligation seriously, that we design with it in mind, and that section 11 sets out precisely what we will do if something does go wrong.

11. What happens if there is a breach

A personal data breach means any unauthorised processing of personal data, or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction or loss of access to it, that compromises its confidentiality, integrity or availability.

If one occurs, the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 impose two separate obligations on us, and we will discharge both.

  • We will tell you. Every affected person will be notified without delay, in clear and plain language, through the application and by the contact details we hold. The notification will describe the nature, extent and timing of the breach, the categories of data involved, the consequences that are likely to be relevant to you, the measures we have taken to mitigate the risk, and the steps we recommend you take to protect yourself.
  • We will tell the regulator. We will inform the Data Protection Board of India of the breach without delay, and will provide the Board with the full particulars — the events and circumstances leading to it, the measures taken to remedy it, the findings on who caused it, and a copy of the notice given to affected persons — within 72 hours, as Rule 7 requires.

We will not delay notifying you while an investigation is still running, on the reasoning that we do not yet know everything. You will be told what we know at the time, and told again when we know more.

13. Cookies, trackers and advertising

The VillKro application uses no cookies, no advertising identifiers, no tracking pixels, and no cross-site or cross-application tracking technology of any kind. The policy pages hosted at api.serveloco.app are plain static HTML documents: they set no cookies, load no third-party scripts, and carry no analytics.
